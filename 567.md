This is a solid landing page — the positioning is sharp and the problem is crystal clear. Here is my honest take on what's working well and where I'd push harder:

**What's strong**
- **The hook is perfect.** "Get paid. Keep the relationship" immediately resolves the core tension freelancers feel. Most collection tools sound aggressive; yours sounds human.
- **The sample email is smart.** Showing the actual draft for "Sarah" builds instant trust. It proves the AI won't sound robotic.
- **Pricing is well-structured.** The free tier (5 invoices) is a genuine taste, not a teaser. The jump to Pro at $19 is low enough that one recovered invoice justifies the cost — that's a compelling mental math moment for users.
- **FAQ is doing real work.** You preempt the three biggest objections: "Will clients know it's automated?", "Can I edit it?", and "What if they already paid?"

**Where I'd tighten up**

1. **Social proof is thin.** You have one anonymous beta quote. Before someone connects their QuickBooks and grants Gmail access, they need to see faces and names. Even 3–4 specific testimonials with headshots, job titles, and a metric ("cut my average collection time from 21 days to 9") would dramatically lift conversion.

2. **The "How it works" section undersells the setup.** "Under 5 minutes" is a great claim, but the three steps are text-only. A 30-second loom-style demo GIF or an embedded video of the actual onboarding flow would crush objections about complexity.

3. **Security/trust signals are missing.** You're asking for QuickBooks (read-only, which is good — say that louder) and Gmail access. Add a small section or badge row: OAuth security, data encryption, read-only QBO access, no invoice data stored longer than necessary, etc. Freelancers are paranoid about their client data.

4. **The "Who uses GentleTap" section is too generic.** "Freelancers & consultants" and "Agencies" are huge buckets. I'd niche down to 2–3 specific personas with mini-stories. For example: "The solo designer invoicing $8K/month" vs. "The 5-person dev shop with 40 open invoices." Specificity sells.

5. **Pro+ WhatsApp feature needs context.** "450 WhatsApp messages/mo" — is that per user? Per team? What happens when they run out? Can they buy more? A small tooltip or one-line explanation would help.

6. **CTA placement.** You have "Early access" once at the top, but no sticky header CTA and no bottom-of-page CTA after the pricing table. Someone who scrolls through the whole page and gets convinced has to scroll back up to act. Add a final CTA block after pricing.

**One strategic thought**
Your biggest competitor isn't other SaaS tools — it's the freelancer's own guilt and procrastination. Lean harder into the emotional relief angle. A line like "Stop rehearsing the 'just checking in' email in your head" speaks to the actual daily pain.

Overall, this looks launch-ready. The copy is warm, the product logic is sound, and the pricing is fair. If you can add stronger social proof and a 60-second demo, I think conversion will jump significantly.
